9e. **Circle/ellipse rasterization changed to the 4-connected rule (user via Gubsy, 2026-09-02):** Gubsy read our circles as "corner to corner" against online generators that "zig zag ... connected on the 90 degree faces". Verified against the reference tool's OWN source (github.com/donatj/Circle-Generator, src/Generators/Circle.ts + src/Math.ts): it has thin/thick/filled modes; its `thin` (exclude when all FOUR orthogonal neighbours are inside) is the 8-connected ring our midpoint rule produced, its `thick` (exclude when all EIGHT are inside) is the 4-connected zig-zag Gubsy wants. Implemented `thick`: Internal/ConicBoundary.cs replaces Internal/MidpointConic.cs (deleted); Circle and Ellipse call sites only, signatures/validation/thickness/clip untouched; Ring inherits it through its two Circle calls. Radius KEEPS meaning extent in blocks — asserted live by a new test over all 384 radius/parity cases.

**The boundary is drawn at r + 0.25, not r, and that constant is load-bearing.** Radii and centre offsets are multiples of 0.5, so every squared distance a block can have is a multiple of 0.25 and so is r-squared: blocks land EXACTLY on the curve and the row collapses. Confirmed independently before accepting: at boundary r, the spec §3 canonical r=0.5 corner 2x2 returns the EMPTY SET, r=1.5 at a corner shrinks 4x4 to 2x2, r=2.5 at a corner shrinks 6 columns to 4. r+0.25 is the unique offset that can never coincide with the lattice ((r+0.25)^2 is 0.0625 mod 0.25); bounding boxes then match the old rasterizer for all 384 circles and all 576 block-centred ellipse pairs. donatj's own r+0.5 parameterisation fails the same way at the other parity. Do not "simplify" this constant.

Consequences to watch (Gubsy's pass, none provable by the harness): (a) counts are 8r exactly at a block centre, ~41% more ghost cubes than before — r=64 goes 364 to 512 per layer, which is why maxCellsPerProjector (ruling 9d) matters more, not less; (b) **two circles closer than 1.5 apart now fuse into one band** (the boundary band is ~1.41 deep, not ~1.0) — inner/outer radius tooltips warn about it, and §10d Global-radius clamping can walk a ring into that state; (c) thin ellipses at half-step centres lose one block at each tip of the long axis (never once both semi-axes are >= 5, never for a circle), pinned by a named test; (d) r=1 at a block centre is now a solid plus, not a hollow one. Suite 21,064 -> 27,784 tests, all green; every rewritten assertion carries its derivation in the test comment.
