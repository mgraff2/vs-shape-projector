A surveyor's instrument for builders. Set it down, dial in a figure, and it casts a luminous guide onto the world — circles, rings, ellipses, rectangles, regular polygons, triangles and spirals, in layers around one shared centre. It places nothing and breaks nothing. It only shows you where, so the round tower is actually round and the moat closes on itself without a single block being counted by hand.

Where it installs Client and server. The server owns the settings, your client draws them, so everyone playing needs it — and everyone sees the same guides.
Game version Vintage Story 1.22.x — tested against every patch release, 1.22.0 through 1.22.7.
Other mods Tested alongside Carry On, Chisel Tools, Farseer and Claims Radar — each on its own and all together, every release.
What it changes Nothing in your world. The guides are light: no substance, no collision, no block ever placed or removed by the projector.
Getting one A crafting recipe — four cupronickel or brass ingots, two cupronickel plates, two Jonas parts, a clear quartz and a temporal gear. Right-click the placed block to open its dial.

The problem it solves

Circles are the obvious one, but the real difficulty is anything that has to be concentric, level, or laid on ground that is not flat. A moat needs two circles a fixed distance apart. A round wall needs the same figure repeated thirty courses up. A hillside path needs a line that stays on the surface as the surface changes. All of that is arithmetic you would otherwise do with a tape of blocks and a lot of recounting.

Seven figures, forty-eight layers, one centre

Figure What you give it
Circle A radius, in half-block steps.
Ring Two radii. It draws both circles and leaves the band between them empty — that band is what you dig.
Ellipse A radius each way. Equal, and you have a circle.
Rectangle Width and depth, edge to edge, with the drawn size always shown so parity never surprises you.
Regular polygon Sides, a corner distance and a rotation. Three sides is a triangle; many is very nearly a circle.
Spiral Turns, the gap between laps, and where it starts.

Every layer shares the projector's centre and keeps its own size, colour, height offset and on/off switch. The centre moves in half-block steps — on a block for an odd diameter, on the seam between two for an even one — and it can sit well away from the projector, so the instrument stands beside the work instead of in the middle of it.

Thickness and height: one layer instead of twenty

A figure can be marked several blocks broad rather than several layers set one apart — the band grows inward, so the outer edge stays exactly where the radius puts it. And a figure can stand several blocks tall, so one layer describes a whole wall instead of one course each. A thirty-block round tower is one layer, not thirty.

Level, or laid on the land

Fixed height One dead-level line whatever the ground does — wall tops, floors, platforms. Where the earth swallows the line, cut down to it; where it hangs in air, fill up to it. That, too, is information.
Follow terrain The line sits on the surface like a rope laid out by hand, and follows the floor down as you excavate, so a dig line is never lost halfway through the digging. Over a slope, a tall layer keeps its height.
Water Yours to rule per layer: the line can rest on the surface and mark the bank, or look through to the bed and mark the bottom.

A worked example: the moat

Set the projector at the centre of a round holding and give it three layers.

  1. A ring, inner radius 20, outer radius 23, following the terrain. This is the moat, three blocks wide, both lines lying on the land wherever it goes. Dig between them; as the pit deepens the lines follow the floor down and the edge is never lost.
  2. A circle, radius 11, following the terrain. The path that rings the house, resting on whatever ground it crosses.
  3. A circle, radius 30, at fixed height, offset to the wall's intended top. Level all the way round — hanging in air where the ground falls away, buried where it rises. Build up to it.

Three figures, one centre, concentric by construction. Nothing was counted.

While you build

Marks turn green Fill a marked block and its guide goes green, so the figure completes in front of you and you can see at a glance what is left.
A holographic miniature The whole design in miniature, floating above the block, so you can read a forty-layer plan without walking it. Switchable on its own, without stopping the projection.
Named presets Saved per player, not per projector, so a shape you worked out once follows you to every projector you own. Load one in place of what is there, or add it on top.
Shortcuts Page Up copies the selected layer one block higher, a course per press. Page Down copies it one block outward, filling toward a disc. + and − grow or shrink every layer at once.
Tooltips that do the arithmetic Hover any control and it tells you what it will do to the layer you have selected — "radius 11 → 12", "Y offset +2 → +3" — not a general description.
Hide it all O hides every projection for you alone. Other players still see theirs.
It remembers Break a configured projector and replace it, or carry it with Carry On, and it keeps its layers.

For server owners

ModConfig/shapeprojector.json sets the ceilings: largest radius (256 by default) and layers per projector (48), the per-projector budget of guide blocks, render distance, and the miniature's size, placement and style. The projector is server-authoritative — a client cannot ask for a figure the server has not agreed to.
